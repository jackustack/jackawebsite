How I Work

Good consulting should make the problem clearer, not make you more dependent on the consultant. I start by understanding the business problem and how the work actually gets done before recommending changes to systems, data, or processes.

Clear scope before the work begins

You should know what I am reviewing, what is included, what is not included, and what you will receive before work starts. If the scope needs to change, we discuss that before additional work is added.

Assessments stand on their own

An assessment should be useful even if you do not hire me to implement the recommendations. Findings are intended to help you understand what is wrong, why it matters, and what should be addressed first.

You can use the findings yourself, work with another provider, or ask me to help implement them.

Understand the process before changing the system

Workarounds often exist for a reason. If people are correcting reports, maintaining spreadsheets, or working around a system, I want to understand what they know before recommending that the workaround be removed or automated.

Your team keeps the knowledge

Documentation, findings, and recommendations are yours. When I make changes, the goal is for your team to understand what changed, why it changed, and how to work with it after the project ends.

No project for the sake of a project

Not every problem requires a rebuild, a new platform, or a larger engagement. If something is working well enough, I will say so. If a smaller fix is the appropriate solution, that is what I will recommend.

Not sure which service fits?

Describe the business problem, what it is affecting, and what you need to accomplish. You do not need to diagnose the technical cause first.
